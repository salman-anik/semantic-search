Sakib is a 21-year-old university student who lives in a rented room in Mirpur, Dhaka, with two other students.
Sakib comes from a small town in Bogura, and his parents and younger sister still live there.
Sakib studies economics and is in the third year of his four-year degree.
Every morning Sakib catches a crowded bus to campus, and the ride takes over an hour when the traffic is bad.
During the monsoon, the roads near Sakib's room fill with water, so he walks through the flooded street or takes a rickshaw.
Sakib eats breakfast at a tea stall near his room, where he usually has paratha, an egg and a cup of tea.
For lunch Sakib often goes to a cheap canteen near campus that serves rice, lentils and fish curry.
Biryani is Sakib's favorite food, and he treats himself to a plate on Friday evenings.
Sakib earns some pocket money by teaching math to two school students at their home in the evenings.
Sakib shares his monthly room rent equally with his roommates, and they split the electricity and gas bills.
Sakib receives money from his father through bKash at the start of each month, and he also pays small bills with the app.
When there is load shedding, Sakib studies by the light of a rechargeable lamp and charges his phone with a power bank.
Sakib's biggest hobby is cricket, and he plays with friends on a nearby field every Friday morning.
Sakib never misses a Bangladesh national team match and watches it on TV at a friend's house.
Before final exams Sakib studies in the university library until it closes, then continues at his desk in his room.
Sakib struggles with statistics, so he joined a study group with three classmates who meet twice a week.
Sakib is a member of the university debate club, which meets every Thursday afternoon to practice public speaking.
Sakib wants to find an internship at a bank or a company in the final year of his studies.
He is learning English by watching videos and reading news so that he can do well in job interviews.
Sakib often feels homesick, and he calls his mother every night after dinner.
Before Eid, Sakib travels home by train, and he must book his ticket days in advance because the seats sell out fast.
During Eid, Sakib visits his relatives, eats his grandmother's sweet vermicelli and receives small gifts from the elders.
On Pohela Boishakh Sakib wears a panjabi, eats panta bhat with fried fish, and walks around the fair with his friends.
Sakib has a small old smartphone, and he is saving money to buy a laptop for his studies.
When Sakib has a cold or fever, he visits a nearby pharmacy and sees a doctor at the local clinic if he does not get better.
Sakib's roommate Imran snores loudly, so Sakib sometimes uses earphones to sleep.
On weekends Sakib sometimes goes to Hatirjheel with friends to enjoy the evening breeze and street snacks.
Sakib's father is a school teacher, and his mother takes care of the home and a small vegetable garden.
Sakib's younger sister Nusrat is in high school, and he helps her with English homework through video calls.
Sakib dreams of working abroad for a few years and then coming back to Bangladesh to start his own business.
